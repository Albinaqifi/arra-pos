Use a separate, marked sample database created with the sample-accounts setup in README, with seeded sample tables/menu and synthetic records. Use the sample waiter for operational configuration, and the combined sample identity for its permitted financial view; sign in and select the same identity where required. Do not record credentials. 1. **Permitted navigation and return:** Sign in as the sample waiter, select that identity, and open Administration from tables. Open Areas and tables, Menu, Employees and Order history using the overview and sidebar; return to the overview from an area, then use Back to tables. Separately sign in and select the combined sample identity and open Financial information and Operational reports where permitted. Expect the chosen area and current-location cue, usable controls, and a return to the table grid without changing saved data. **Evidence:** the independent renderer test exercised both navigation callbacks for all six areas, current cues and area-to-overview returns (passed 1/1); it did not exercise real Electron, service-backed area content, or Back to tables.
2. **Switch identity and deny access:** While signed in as the sample waiter and in Administration, choose Switch user and select a different sample identity. Open a configuration area that identity cannot access, and also try Employees while the selected identity differs from the signed-in account. Expect the selection screen and updated identity; the restricted area shows an understandable denial, with no edit forms or false empty-history claims. Direct protected requests must also be denied. **Evidence:** the bridge/session test exercised clearing and reselecting the same sample identity, and direct permission tests exercised denied requests; the configuration renderer test exercised mocked denied responses for three pages (all passed in the recorded 11/11). The different-identity desktop flow was not exercised.
3. **Stale-data isolation:** With synthetic records visible under an allowed identity, switch to a less-privileged identity, reopen the previously viewed area, and, where the test harness can control timing, release a delayed response from the prior identity after the switch. Expect no prior identity's protected records, totals, forms or history to appear; the new identity sees only fresh authorized results or a denial. Repeat across separately signed-in A and B sample owners for financial records. **Evidence:** the bridge/session test checked that table sections were cleared on switching away and reloaded on reselecting the same identity. The cross-identity, delayed-response and financial-screen scenarios were not exercised.
